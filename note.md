Answer for question 4a-4c = Both `customer_id` and `customer_unique_id` look like customer identifiers, but `customer_id` is generated per order (99,441 distinct values, equal to the number of orders), while `customer_unique_id` identifies the person (96,096 distinct values), so customers should be counted with `COUNT(DISTINCT customer_unique_id)`. `order_items` is the junction table: its composite key (`order_id`, `order_item_id`) resolves the many-to-many relationships between orders and products and between orders and sellers. The data is stricter than my diagram on customers → orders: I drew "one or many" orders per customer, but every `customer_id` has exactly one order (min = max = 1), because a returning person receives a new `customer_id` each time. The circle on orders → order_items is justified by the data: 775 orders have no items, mostly `unavailable` (603) and `canceled` (164).
